# Bench qualification

Qualification establishes observed facts for one fixture and one target. It does
not make a vendor default, an adapter label, or a passing software test a safety
claim. The resulting evidence supports an operator's `Authority::Commission`
request and a later bounded grant; it never authorizes a different fixture or
another physical target.

The current request shape is generated by `cheirismos schema`. Keep machine
inputs in that generated schema and the typed request API, not copied JSON in
this runbook. The relevant boundaries are the [service request API](https://github.com/forkwright/cheirismos/blob/main/src/service/api.rs),
[commissioned profile and grant types](https://github.com/forkwright/cheirismos/blob/main/src/domain/mod.rs), and
[supervisor-owned instrument configuration](https://github.com/forkwright/cheirismos/blob/main/src/instruments/config.rs).
The implementation contract remains [IMPLEMENTATION.md](IMPLEMENTATION.md).

## Bench boundary

Use a sacrificial NOR board, I2C responder/loopback, UART loopback, resistive
ADC source, and an electronic load or equivalent isolated PSU fixture. They
must be electrically separate from a target under investigation. Record the
fixture revision, cable and adapter identifiers, physical pin map, measured
supply voltage, current limit, and write-protect state before connecting a
candidate target.

The bench must have an independently observed disconnect path. A relay command,
Bus Pirate status, or a green USB LED is not evidence that target power,
write-protect, or a relay contact is in the expected physical state. Record the
meter, continuity tester, or logic-analyzer observation that establishes each
of those facts.

## Instrument qualification

### Bus Pirate BPIO2

1. Enumerate both CDC interfaces. Record the exact USB identity, interface
   identity, and selected device path; bind the BPIO2 `SerialBinding` only to
   the reviewed second CDC interface. A matching vendor or product ID alone is
   insufficient.
2. Acquire a fresh BPIO2 status response and record negotiated FlatBuffer,
   hardware, and firmware versions, firmware hash/date when reported, available
   modes, transfer ceilings, ADC observations, GPIO state, and PSU state.
   Confirm the expected BPIO2 protocol framing by measured request/response
   traffic, including a rejected malformed frame. Do not infer support from a
   shell banner or product name.
3. On the sacrificial fixture, prove the configured SPI, I2C, and UART speeds
   with an instrument that can observe the bus. Measure GPIO mapping at the
   fixture connector, then compare it with the `pin_map`; names in a map are
   not wiring evidence.
4. Measure ADC readings against at least two independently measured known
   voltages. Exercise PSU enable, setpoint, current limit, and disable only into
   the isolated load. Record observed voltage/current and the shutdown result.
5. For SPI NOR, read JEDEC and SFDP, bind the observed geometry, then read,
   erase, write a known artifact, and verify readback on the sacrificial part.
   Test write-protect with the adapter at the actual measured voltage. Do not
   carry this profile to another flash part merely because its capacity matches.
6. For I2C, use the commissioned responder at its recorded address and prove
   an expected read/write transcript. For UART, use the loopback, transmit a
   bounded test payload, and capture no more than the approved bound. A scan,
   raw transfer, or transmit is an effect, not a harmless probe.

### Numato relay

For each named relay mapping, record the controller identity and firmware,
then compare `relay readall` with independently measured continuity at the
physical common, normally-open, and normally-closed contacts. Repeat with the
relay board unpowered and through a power-loss/recovery cycle. The controller's
logical coil state is useful evidence but does not prove contact state, default
position, fixture continuity, or target power isolation.

### UVC capture

Record the UVC device's complete USB identity from sysfs ancestry and the
reviewed capture executable digest. Capture exactly one bounded frame with the
configured timeout and byte bound, preserve the raw artifact, and record its
container-level result. The adapter checks a PNG container signature and device
identity; it does not establish what the image depicts or that a visual target
is safe to manipulate.

## Configuration is a composite effect

Default observation performs no instrument configuration. Every bus read or
write that needs a mode, speed, GPIO direction, or PSU state carries an
explicit **InstrumentConfigure** effect in addition to its apparent bus
operation. Admission requires all of the following for that composite effect:

- a qualified instrument binding and current fixture fingerprint;
- a reviewed plan and an unrevoked grant that permits both the capability and
  each effect;
- a budget reservation covering configuration, transfer, and postcondition
  observation; and
- the configured electrical and region limits.

A reconciliation request does not replay an unknown actuation. A reviewed
reconciliation policy may reapply the exact previously reviewed configuration
only to obtain a postcondition read, and its evidence must say that it does not
prove the original actuation. Without that policy, a missing required mode is a
partial or unknown result. This follows the backend's configuration and
reconciliation boundary rather than treating status as proof of a safe setup.

## Fault matrix before a target grant

Exercise these faults on the sacrificial fixture and preserve the observation
or receipt for each. A grant for a target is blocked until the recorded result
matches the expected conservative outcome.

| Injected condition | Required observed outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Selected CDC endpoint removed or swapped | Identity/path failure; no operation reaches another endpoint. |
| BPIO2 protocol/version or frame limit differs | Rejection before bus transfer. |
| Bus mode or configured speed differs | No normal read/write without reviewed InstrumentConfigure authority. |
| NOR JEDEC/SFDP, geometry, or write-protect differs | No erase/write; identity evidence explains the refusal. |
| I2C responder absent or UART loopback open | Bounded failure/partial evidence, never a synthetic success. |
| ADC source or PSU load differs from recorded fact | Mismatch evidence; no target-power inference. |
| Numato USB loss, relay power loss, or continuity mismatch | Logical state remains distinct from physical contact evidence. |
| UVC identity, executable digest, frame deadline, or byte bound fails | No frame claim or unbounded subprocess output. |
| Supervisor interruption during an effect | Durable attempt remains unknown until fresh reconciliation evidence. |

## Commissioning checklist

Before issuing a target grant, the operator records:

- fixture revision and commissioning session;
- target fingerprint and target-local evidence;
- each instrument's exact identity, configuration fingerprint, and qualified
  capabilities;
- measured electrical limits, adapter voltage, write-protect behavior, and
  physical pin/contact map;
- passed fault-matrix evidence and the known limitations of every observation;
- a reviewed profile with a distinct reviewer, plus a grant with expiry,
  capabilities, effect permissions, and remaining budget; and
- a safe return state: target power isolated, PSU disabled, relays measured,
  and no unresolved attempt silently treated as complete.

## Reusing the bench for a second target

A second target gets a new commissioned profile. Reuse only the bench fixture
facts that were measured unchanged: instrument identity, adapter voltage,
isolation arrangement, and the physical mapping. Re-observe the second target,
create a new target fingerprint, record target-specific preconditions and NOR
identity, and repeat the target-relevant fault matrix. Do not copy the first
target's grant, target fingerprint, evidence digest, or write permission.
